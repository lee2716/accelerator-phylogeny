# Pre-registration — accel_trends

**Written:** 2026-10-09, before any labelling, enrichment or source collection. **Write-once**: later
changes are appended below the line `## Amendments` with a date; nothing above it is edited
(`scripts/validate.py prereg` checks that everything above `## Amendments` still matches the hash in `docs/prereg_head.sha256`).

## Arms

| Arm | What | Source |
|---|---|---|
| E | DAC / DATE / ICCAD / ASP-DAC 2018–2025 (2026 partial, never in a slope) | the corpus index named by `corpus_index` in `config.local.json` |
| A | ISCA / MICRO / HPCA / ASPLOS / ISSCC / JSSC 2018–2025, metadata + abstracts | OpenAlex |
| I | industry product generations and roadmaps since 2018 | web sources, tiered |
| B | accepted long-form technical writing, any language | web sources, tiered |

All trend quantities are **shares of an arm-year**, with Wilson 95% intervals. Counts appear only as `n`.

## Thresholds fixed now

| Quantity | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| classifier agreement to use an axis | Cohen's kappa >= 0.70 between two independently worded prompts (>= 0.80 for the relevance gate) | below this a cell boundary is noise |
| cell shown solid | n >= 10; below that hatched with n printed | 3 papers must not look like 300 |
| rising / falling | permutation p < 0.05 over 1000 year-shuffles AND absolute change >= 3 share points | ~250 slice papers per edition: 1 point is 2–3 papers |
| citation-edge recall vs full-text reference lists | >= 0.80 on 20 papers, else edges are labelled with the measured recall | OpenAlex undercounts some works |
| industry axis cells `unknown` | <= 50% in wave 1, else arm I degrades to timeline + bets | partial disclosure |

## Positive and no-op controls (the pipeline is broken if these fail)

- **Positive:** in arm E, the share of `A0_workload in {transformer_encoder_vit, llm_autoregressive}` rises 2018→2025 and `cnn` falls. expected: transformer+LLM share 2025 minus 2018 >= +10 points; cnn <= -10 points. if not: the labeller or the slice is wrong; no other trend is reported until explained.
- **No-op:** the year-shuffled table. expected: fewer than 5% of cells pass the rising/falling test. if not: the test is miscalibrated and no cell is called rising.

## Hypotheses (each: expected / if not)

- **Q1 lag.** Transformer/attention workloads reach 2% share in arm A before arm E. expected: arm A leads by 1–3 years. if not: EDA venues track the workload as fast as architecture venues and "EDA lags" is dropped from the narrative.
- **Q2 device base of in-memory computing.** expected: among arm-E CIM papers, RRAM is the most common device in 2018–2020 and SRAM in 2023–2025. if not: report the measured order; the "shift to SRAM/digital" story is not supported in these venues.
- **Q2b digital vs analog CIM.** expected: `cim_digital` share of CIM papers rises by >= 10 points from 2018–2020 to 2023–2025. if not: digital CIM is not overtaking analog in these venues.
- **Q3 scope.** expected: >= 60% of arm-E CIM papers are `macro_circuit` or `engine_core` scope and < 15% are `cluster_tile` or larger. if not: the macro-versus-system gap is not visible in abstracts.
- **Q4 rung.** expected: >= 60% of arm-E CIM papers with a stated rung are `simulator` or `analytical`. if not: report the distribution; "CIM growth is simulator-rung growth" is dropped.
- **Q5 export.** expected: < 15% of out-of-corpus citers of slice papers have an ML-venue primary topic. if not: the upstream/downstream framing is revised.
- **Q6 upstream-not-arrived.** At least 3 of ~40 census terms have >= 50 upstream works and <= 2 slice papers after the synonym check. if not: the EDA literature already tracks ML techniques closely and gap type G3 is retired.

## Gap patterns (generated by script; a gap is a hypothesis, not an idea)

G1 academic-only: >= 2% share for >= 3 years in E or A, no product generation with that value (basis != unknown).
G2 industry-only: value disclosed in >= 3 generations across >= 2 vendors, < 1% share in the slice.
G3 upstream-not-arrived: from Q6.
G4 open bet: a bet open for >= 3 years with no node that settles it.
G5 dead line: share below its 2018 level, last in-corpus successor >= 3 years ago, and an upstream condition changed.
G6 adjacent hole: empty axis x axis cell whose expected count under independence is >= 8 and observed <= 1.

Filters, in order, each with a typed outcome and never omitted: F1 not vacuous · F2 not foreclosed by physics or economics · F3 not tried-and-failed (a miss is `NOT_CHECKABLE_80PCT_CLOSED`, never "nobody tried") · F4 not already shipped or settled · F5 reachable with the toolchain at hand, with `expected:`/`if not:` on the card.

## Kill criteria

K1 (after the thin slice): gate kappa < 0.80 or A1/A2 kappa < 0.70 after one prompt revision → axes are not readable from abstracts; degrade to a word-bounded keyword timeline and say so on the page.
K2: median in-corpus forward citation edges for 2018–2022 slice papers < 1 → forward lineage is re-based on the out-of-corpus hop.
K3: > 50% of wave-1 industry axis cells `unknown` → arm I becomes timeline + bets.
K4: <= 2 of the idea cards pass F1–F4 → the mechanism does not produce ideas; the page is kept as a reading tool and says so.

## Decisions fixed before the first run

1. Arm A (architecture and circuits venues) is a labelled arm, not only a set of cited neighbours.
2. OpenAlex is queried without `mailto` (no e-mail address is sent to third parties); slower anonymous pool.
3. No Wayback snapshots are created; a source keeps URL, access date, SHA-256 of the fetched page, summary and facts.
4. Every result row carries a content hash of `scripts/`, so a row is traceable even from an uncommitted tree.
5. A multi-label `lever` axis (tiling, mapping, interconnect, non-ideality …) records what a paper's contribution attacks.
6. Page data ships as `web/data.js` (a script assigning one global), not `fetch()` of JSON, so the page opens from `file://`.
7. A 30-abstract human-labelled gold set is optional; without it the page header reads "classifier checked against itself only".

## Amendments

**2026-10-09 (before any label was read).** Token budget is limited, so two changes to how labels are produced; neither changes a threshold.
1. Classifier calls run one at a time, never in parallel. Each batch is a file on disk, so stopping the run loses at most the batch in flight.
2. The second, independently worded prompt (variant B) is applied to a fixed sample instead of every paper: batches 000 and 001 of each slice (170 papers in the in-memory slice). Agreement between the two prompts is computed on that sample and reported with its n. Consequence: per-paper agreement is known only for the sample; papers outside it carry a single label.

**2026-10-09, correction to item 1 above (still before any label was read).** At most three classifier calls run at the same time (not strictly one). Everything else in that amendment stands.

**2026-10-09, two derived classifications (definitions fixed before any value was computed).**

*Influence* — rank of a paper's OpenAlex citation count among all papers of the same venue and year in the
four-venue corpus (not only the labelled slice). Tiers: `top_10`, `top_25` (10–25%), `upper_half` (25–50%),
`lower_half`, and `too_early` for papers of 2025 or later, whatever their count. Raw counts are never
compared across years.

*Novelty by precedence* — for each paper, two combinations are formed from its labels: (compute paradigm ×
workload target) and (compute paradigm × first lever). A combination with a `not_stated`, `other`,
`generic_dnn` or `target_generic` member is ignored. For each combination, over the labelled slice through 2025:
its first year and its total count. A paper takes the strongest tier any of its combinations gives:
`opened_line` (published in the combination's first year, that year is after 2018, and the combination
reached ≥ 5 papers), `early_entrant` (within one year after the first year, combination ≥ 5), `singleton`
(combination has ≤ 2 papers), `follows_established` (otherwise), and `predates_data` when every usable
combination was already present in 2018, the first year of data. Papers with no usable combination are
`unclassifiable`.

What this is not: it is precedence inside four venues, not world priority; it says who came first, not who
was right; and it inherits the labels' error. expected: fewer than 10% of papers are `opened_line`.
if not: the combinations are too fine-grained to mean anything and the tiers are reported as unreliable.
